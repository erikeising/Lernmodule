2 · Working

22 minutes
Task 2
« Sort the nine lines below into three groups. You decide the groups — but you must be able to say what your groups have in common. Some lines might seem to fit more than one group: pick the one that fits best and note why in the box underneath. »
Operator: classify / justify (AFB II)

Now that you have sorted the lines yourself — here are the three modes of persuasion the lines belong to, and why.

Ethos — credibility

Lines 1, 6, 9. An appeal to the speaker's authority, experience, or trustworthiness.

Pathos — emotion

Lines 2, 4, 8. An appeal to the audience's feelings, hopes, or fears.

Logos — logic

Lines 3, 5, 7. An appeal to facts, numbers, and rational argument.

The line doing two jobs: line 2 contains a statistic ("every ninety seconds") — but the statistic is there to make you feel something, not to support an inference. That makes it pathos dressed up as logos.

Borrowed credibility: line 9 is ethos, but it isn't the speaker's own credibility — it is borrowed from an outside authority.

Compare this to what you wrote above. Where did you sort differently — and why might that be? You don't need to write anything down for this; just notice it.

Sentence frame: "The line appeals to the audience's … by … . The effect is that the reader/listener …"

Modelled example (a different line, so you can't copy it)

"The line about the receipt (line 8) relies on a small, concrete detail rather than a general claim. By specifying that the receipt has been kept for thirty years, the speaker invites the reader to picture a real, ordinary person, which creates a sense of quiet loyalty. The effect is that the product's value feels proven by lived experience, not just claimed."
